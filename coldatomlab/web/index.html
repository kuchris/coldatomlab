      THE 2D WORKSPACE MODEL
      
      
      One wave. Many atoms.
      
      
      The separate 3D expansion model evolves all three axes with complete trap release.
      
      
      
        We evolve a dilute, weakly interacting condensate with an effective
        two-dimensional Gross–Pitaevskii equation:
      
      
      
      i ∂ψ/∂t = [−½∇² + V + g|ψ|²]ψ
      
      
      
        The wavefunction has unit norm: ∫|ψ|² dx dy = 1. The interaction
        coefficient g includes the atom number. Density is normalized
        probability per area in dimensionless mode. Laboratory mode displays the
        planar number density N|ψ|²/a₀² in atoms/μm².
      
      
      
      
        Lengths use a₀ = √(ℏ/mω₀), time uses 1/ω₀, and energies use ℏω₀.
        Laboratory mode selects a species, atom number, scattering length and
        axial confinement, derives g, and converts the axes to μm, ms and E/h in
        Hz. Numerical export arrays retain these dimensionless conventions.
      
      
      
      
        Single cloud: normalized imaginary-time preparation in a harmonic
        trap. Two clouds: a normalized, coherent sum of two Gaussian
        packets with a controlled relative phase, initially released. This is an
        idealized initial state, not a simulation of splitting or condensation.
      
      
      
      
        Split / hold / release: begin with one trapped ground state. A
        smoothstep ramp raises a Gaussian barrier. During Hold, a smooth
        left/right bias changes the relative phase; the sequence then removes
        all in-plane potential and stops after the chosen expansion time. Stage
        durations are rounded to solver steps; the timeline shows the actual
        boundaries. Energy changes when the applied potential changes.
      
      
      
      
        Left and right populations share the x = 0 column equally. Mirror phase
        compares the two mirrored half-planes and is not a fitted experimental
        phase. Fringe spacing and contrast are conservative estimates from at
        least three regular profile peaks; they are unavailable when the pattern
        is unresolved. Pin reference retains a snapshot for comparison and
        exports both runs together.
      
      
      
      
        Real-time evolution uses a second-order split-step Fourier method.
        Periodic boundaries require enough empty space around the cloud.
        Evolution stops when more than 0.1% of the norm reaches the outer
        boundary band, or at t = 20.
      
      
      
      
        Density colors use a linear, frame-relative scale with a labeled
        maximum. Phase colors are cyclic; pixels below 0.1% of the current peak
        density are masked. The phase is a model diagnostic, not directly an
        absorption image.
      
      
      
      
        This model retains tight transverse confinement. It does not include
        laser cooling, a thermal cloud, losses, camera noise, or full
        three-dimensional expansion.
      
      
      
      
        Numerical resolution matters. Compare smaller time steps, finer grids,
        and larger domains before drawing quantitative conclusions.
