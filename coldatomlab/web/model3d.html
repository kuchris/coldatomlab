MODEL / CONVENTIONS / SOURCES

A numerical condensate in three dimensions

This experiment evolves a complex field on an x/y/z grid. All three trap axes are removed on release; contact interactions remain. It is a zero-temperature mean-field calculation, not a reconstruction of a particular experimental image.

Scales and normalization

ω₀ = 2πf₀; a₀ = √(ℏ / mω₀); τ = ω₀t; Xᵢ = rᵢ/a₀
∫ |ψ|² dX dY dZ = 1; g₃D = 4πNaₛ/a₀
i ∂ψ/∂τ = [−½∇² + V + g₃D|ψ|²] ψ
V = ½ Σᵢ (fᵢ/f₀)² Xᵢ² before release; V = 0 after release

N uses the usual N−1 ≈ N convention. The mass is Rb-87. Number density is N|ψ|²/a₀³. A projection along z is N/a₀² ∫|ψ|²dZ; its image-plane integral returns the atom number. A central slice has volume-density units and is not a projection. Energy is per condensate atom, displayed as E/h in Hz.

Preparation and numerical evolution

Interacting ground states use normalized imaginary time, exact local nonlinear substeps and spectral kinetic propagation. The preparation panel reports iteration count, step, stationary residual and runtime. Noninteracting ground states start from the analytic oscillator. Real-time Strang splitting uses the original full grid and never renormalizes the state. The simulation step is independent of display frame rate.

The Fourier box is periodic. The outer band (at least two cells; otherwise one-sixteenth of the side) is monitored. Evolution stops at the first step where its probability exceeds 0.1%; this guard is not a rigorous error bound. Refine time step, grid, box and preparation step before interpreting a quantitative result. Norm conservation alone does not demonstrate accuracy.

Independent theory comparisons

Ωᵢ = fᵢ/f₀
g = 0: σᵢ(τ) = √[(1 + Ωᵢ²τ²) / (2Ωᵢ)]
TF chemical potential: μTF = ½ [15g ΩxΩyΩz / (4π)]^(2/5)
TF initial RMS: σᵢ(0) = √(2μTF) / (√7 Ωᵢ)
Full-release scaling: bᵢ″ = Ωᵢ² / (bᵢ bx by bz), bᵢ(0)=1, bᵢ′(0)=0
Absolute TF RMS: σᵢ(τ) = σᵢ(0)bᵢ(τ)

The TF scaling ODE is solved separately from the 3D field. It does not drive the visualization. The table distinguishes the absolute TF prediction from scaling anchored to the numerical initial widths. Those are different comparisons. When interactions are zero, the exact Gaussian reference replaces TF.

TF requires chemical potential large compared with all three trap quanta and a small initial kinetic contribution. The displayed ratios are indicators, not experimental certification. Finite kinetic energy and cloud tails cause legitimate GPE–TF differences even when the numerical calculation has converged.

What is visible

The rotatable surface uses marching tetrahedra on the numerical density, block averaged to a smaller rendering grid when indicated. Its threshold is a fraction of that displayed grid's peak. The box stays fixed during evolution. Central planes and column densities use the full solver grid. These panels are ideal model observables. The separate virtual camera below them forms absorption images from the same field. Thermal clouds, loss, trap imperfections and apparatus calibration are omitted.

Established primary references

Castin & Dum, Physical Review Letters 77, 5315 (1996): anisotropic Thomas–Fermi scaling and complete release. Dalfovo et al., Reviews of Modern Physics 71, 463 (1999): trapped BEC mean-field theory, TF equilibrium, expansion and finite-kinetic-energy corrections. The repository's docs/3D_REFERENCE.md maps the equations to these sources; docs/MODEL3D.md records measured implementation checks. Neither paper validates this software.

Coherent interferometry

The analytic pair is a normalized sum of two noninteracting Gaussians with a controlled right-minus-left phase. It starts released. The dynamic sequence instead prepares one interacting ground state, raises a Gaussian barrier H exp(−X²/(2b²)), holds it with a bias B tanh(X/b)/2, then releases every axis. Split uses a sin² ramp; bias acts only during Hold. Stage times are rounded to complete solver steps and the external potential is evaluated at step midpoints. Positive bias raises the right well and approximately accumulates negative relative phase.

Line density integrates both transverse directions. The phase readout is the argument of the mirror overlap conj(ψ(−X,Y,Z)) ψ(X,Y,Z) for X>0, not a camera-derived fit; it is hidden when coherence or population is too small. Fringe spacing requires at least three resolved local peaks. Its envelope-sensitive value can differ from the analytic carrier period. Pin preserves each record's settings, scale and time. Single-cloud Gaussian and TF width references do not apply to these modes.

Shin et al., PRL 92, 050405 (2004) motivates the splitting, controlled phase and release sequence. Our Rb-87 harmonic-plus-barrier teaching model does not reproduce the paper's sodium apparatus, optical potential, measured image or finite-temperature phase statistics. The projection panels remain ideal densities; the separate virtual camera models optical measurement. The repository's docs/INTERFEROMETER3D.md specifies the conventions and checks.

Three-axis absorption camera

Pause or finish the Camera-friendly pair example, then capture its actual column density along x, y or z. The detector axes are shown on each image. Capture freezes the field; it never advances time or changes normalization. Browser JavaScript performs saturation-corrected Rb-87 absorption, Gaussian intensity blur, pixel-area integration, then seeded independent Poisson photon and Gaussian read noise in atom/reference/dark frames. Invalid counts remain invalid and negative density estimates are retained. 

n_col = N integral |psi|² dX_los / a0²  [atoms / µm²]
sigma0 n_col = -ln(T) + s(1-T); sigma0 = 3 lambda²/(2 pi)
n_est = [ln(R/A) + s(R-A)/R0] / sigma0
lambda = 0.780241209 µm; s = I0/Isat; Isat = 16.69 W/m²

A and R are dark-subtracted electrons; R0 is the expected reference count. Fit the camera and ideal profiles independently with a Gaussian envelope multiplying a quadratic baseline plus b cos(kx) + c sin(kx). Image phase atan2(c,b) refers to cos(kx − phase) at coordinate zero; it is not the source mirror phase. Period is 2 pi/k and fitted contrast is hypot(b,c) divided by the fitted envelope baseline at its center. No theoretical/source phase is supplied to the image fit.

At least 20 valid samples, 2.5 cycles across the ROI and four pixels per period are required. Further residual, contrast and signal gates reject unreliable fits. These are demonstration criteria, not confidence intervals. Looking along x integrates the x-fringes away. Strong blur, coarse pixels, low light or an incompatible envelope can make measurements unavailable. Fitted contrast is an empirical ratio and may slightly exceed one through estimation error. ROI atom counts require every ROI pixel to be valid.

The ideal two-level saturation relation follows Reinaudi et al. (2007), with calibration factor alpha=1. For established imaging context see Ketterle, Durfee & Stamper-Kurn (1999). Gaussian optics and detector settings are teaching assumptions. Recoil, motion during exposure, defocus, optical pumping, multiple scattering, detector saturation and experimental calibration are omitted. High optical depth, underresolved PSF and more than five mean absorbed photons per atom trigger warnings. A good fit does not remove those physical limitations.

Pin and export preserve the complete source and all camera frames/settings. The same replay command independently verifies the source and regenerates camera arrays with numerical tolerances; source GPU comparison is not bitwise replay. The repository's docs/IMAGING3D.md gives formulas, fitting gates, seed convention, tolerances and separate browser/numerical evidence.

Automated phase scans

Measure a phase curve runs fresh WebGPU evolutions for known pair phases, interacting hold times or hold biases. Each completed field is frozen for repeated virtual detector exposures; the manually prepared experiment is preserved. Pause/cancel take effect between preparation, step batches and exposures. Cancel retains completed points. Numerical boundary/norm stops and rejected image fits remain visible as failures.

Fixed teaching recipes use 2,000 Rb-87 atoms and frequencies x/y/z = 30/42/21 Hz, reference 30 Hz. Pair calibration uses zero interactions, separation 6 a₀, a 32 a₀ box and evolution time 3/ω₀. Dynamic scans use scattering length 5.3 nm, a 24 a₀ box, Gaussian barrier height 12 ℏω₀ and width 0.8 a₀, split time 3/ω₀ and expansion 2/ω₀. Actual hold duration is rounded to numerical steps and exported. The isolated-well guide −bias × actual hold time neglects coupling and interaction imbalance; it does not constrain the image fit. These Rb-87 recipes are inspired by Shin et al. (2004), not a reproduction of their sodium experiment.

Circular mean = atan2(mean sin φ, mean cos φ). Circular scatter = sqrt(−2 ln R), where R is the magnitude of that mean vector. Means are unavailable when R < 0.1; scatter also requires two valid exposures. Differences wrap to ±π. Bars describe valid detector exposures, not confidence intervals or thermal/quantum condensate phase fluctuations. Rejected fits contribute to the failure rate; surviving fits may be biased.

Optional 64³/128³ runs keep box, timing and physical settings fixed. Width/norm differences describe the field; ideal-image phase differences also include sampling and estimator sensitivity. Native detector pixels become smaller with finer grids. Two grids are a sensitivity check, not a convergence proof. Camera noise is shown separately.

CSV exports the summary. Compact JSON retains recipes, actual timing, source projections/widths/norm and raw images, but omits complex fields. The replay command independently evolves CPU fields and checks projected observables, camera frames and statistics. Projection/width relative tolerances are 0.5%, with a 0.1% norm-drift limit. Stopped runs lack saved projections and are explicitly unverified. See docs/SCANS3D.md in the repository for the complete contract and measured evidence.

WebGPU execution

The browser GPU backend uses complex float32 and workgroup radix-two FFTs on 32³, 64³ or 128³ grids. Preparation stops at a 3e-6 L2 iterate-change threshold; its stationary residual is measured separately. Real-time evolution is never renormalized. A 0.1% norm-drift guard stops the run. More, smaller time steps can accumulate more floating-point round-off, so a smaller step is not automatically more accurate in float32. Use the float64 CPU reference for precision work.

The standalone WebGPU lab requires hardware WebGPU over HTTPS or localhost. It performs all preparation and evolution in the browser, with no simulation API. GPU exports have their own schema; the replay command compares them against the independent CPU reference with explicit tolerances, not exact GPU replay.

Reproduce a run

Pause, export the complete complex wavefunction and preparation/release settings, then run: 

uv run python -m coldatomlab.replay path/to/coldatomlab-3d-run.json

Replay recomputes the preparation and evolution; it compares the final complex field with the saved array. Preparation takes time again. Performance labels count persistent numerical arrays, not peak process memory.
