MODEL / ASSUMPTIONS / SOURCES

A small quantum system, solved in its full occupation basis.

The state is Σ cn|n, N − n⟩, with Σ|cn|² = 1. The two spatial modes are left and right; the many-body state has N + 1 amplitudes. Probabilities and all plots come from those amplitudes.

H/h = −J(a†LaR + a†RaL) + U(nL − N/2)² − δ(nL − N/2)

J, U and δ are frequencies in Hz, not angular frequencies. Evolution uses exp(−i 2π (H/h) t), with t in seconds. Positive δ raises the right well. U is the on-site pair coefficient in U/2 [nL(nL−1) + nR(nR−1)]; a constant energy offset is removed. No N factor is absorbed into U.

What the measurements do and do not mean

Coherence C is a first-order moment. It is not a single-shot fringe fit, a phase probability distribution or the visibility of a simulated camera image. Phase is unavailable for C ≤ 10⁻⁸. An exact fixed-count state can have no first-order coherence even though individual interference measurements require a more detailed measurement model.

The number-noise ratio is Var(NL) / [N p(1−p)], with p = ⟨NL⟩/N at the displayed time. It is unavailable at fully occupied or empty wells. A ratio below one indicates a narrower distribution than the corresponding binomial reference; this alone is not an entanglement or metrological spin-squeezing certificate.

At J = 0, atom-count probabilities stay fixed while interactions can change coherence through relative phases between occupation amplitudes. Closed finite systems can revive: this is unitary evolution, not irreversible environmental decoherence.

Numerics and physical limits

The browser diagonalizes the real symmetric Hamiltonian with float64 Jacobi rotations, checks residuals and orthogonality, then propagates spectrally from the initial state. There is no per-step renormalization. Run displays 200 equally spaced physical times; frame rate changes playback speed only. Export retains complete final amplitudes, settings, history and seeded counts, plus any pinned reference. Python replay uses an independent NumPy eigensolver.

These fixed spatial orbitals have no 3D shape dynamics. J and U are illustrative inputs, not extracted from the existing GPE cloud. Higher modes, changing orbital shapes, finite temperature, loss, detector noise and apparatus calibration are omitted. The Gaussian number-narrow state is prescribed, not prepared by a simulated splitting ramp. Numerical accuracy within this model does not prove the two-mode approximation applies to an experimental apparatus.
